01

How flow cytometry works

The physics you need to explain a strange dot plot to a lab director, in the order the instrument actually does it: fluid, light, signal, plot.

1.1 · The three subsystems

Every flow cytometer — BriCyte included — is three systems working in sequence. The fluidics system carries stained cells one at a time past a laser. The optics system shines lasers on them and collects the light that comes back. The electronics system turns that light into numbers and hands them to the software. Understanding a strange result almost always means asking which of the three is misbehaving.

1.2 · Fluidics: hydrodynamic focusing

Sample is injected into the centre of a fast-moving, laminar sheath flow. Because the sheath stream narrows as it approaches the laser, the sample core is squeezed into a thin single-file thread of cells — this is hydrodynamic focusing. A faster sheath flow makes the sample core narrower (fewer cells side-by-side, better resolution but slower throughput); a slower sheath flow lets the core widen (faster events/second, but coincident cells can be miscounted). On the BriCyte, this is the “Low / Med / High” flow-rate setting: Low ≈ 10 μL/min, Med ≈ 50 μL/min, High ≈ 100 μL/min — use Low for anything where resolution matters more than speed (QC beads, compensation controls).

Field tip

Air bubbles or a sample tube left to run dry are the single most common cause of unstable fluidics — they show up as a sudden shift or scatter in every plot at once, not just one population. Re-run rather than re-gate.

1.3 · Optics: scatter and fluorescence

As a cell crosses the focused laser spot, it scatters light and, if it carries fluorescent antibody, emits fluorescence at a longer wavelength than the exciting light (the Stokes shift). Two scatter signals are collected on every cytometer:

  • Forward scatter (FSC) — collected close to the laser’s axis (roughly 1–10.5° on BriCyte). Roughly tracks cell size; used to separate debris from intact cells.
  • Side scatter (SSC) — collected at ~90° to the beam. Tracks internal complexity/granularity — this is what separates lymphocytes, monocytes and granulocytes on a CD45‑SSC plot even before any antibody is considered.

Fluorescence is filtered through band-pass filters (named by centre/width, e.g. 530/30 passes 515–545 nm) onto dedicated detectors, one per colour channel.

1.4 · Compensation

Fluorochrome emission spectra overlap — some FITC photons always leak into the PE detector, and vice versa. Compensation mathematically subtracts that spillover so each detector reports only its own dye. BriCyte does this automatically during QC/auto‑setup against the QC beads, but it can be reviewed or fine-tuned per sample on the Compensation tab (input range 0–200%). A panel that looks “smeared” diagonally on a two-colour plot, rather than in tight rectangular quadrants, is almost always a compensation problem, not a biological one.

1.5 · Gating

Gating isolates the population you care about by drawing a region around it and asking the software to only look inside that region on the next plot. The standard sequence for this panel is: scatter/CD45 (find lymphocytes) → CD3 vs CD4 and CD3 vs CD8 (quadrant/cross gates for T‑cell subsets). Section 7 covers exactly how to verify each gate is sitting correctly before a report goes out.

1.6 · Pulse height, width and area

Each detector doesn’t just report one number — it reports a pulse as a cell crosses the beam. Height is the peak signal; area is roughly height × width and approximates total light collected; width tracks how long the cell took to cross the beam (its time of flight). Width is the classic tool for spotting doublets — two cells stuck together give a wider pulse than one cell, even when their total fluorescence (area) looks similar.

The T-cell panel

CD3‑FITC/CD8‑PE/CD45‑PerCP/CD4‑APC Reagent — a single four-colour cocktail that identifies T lymphocytes and their two major subsets in one tube.

3.1 · What each marker is doing

MarkerMWWhat it identifies
CD320–30 kDaε chain of the T‑cell receptor complex — defines the T‑lymphocyte population
CD455 kDaHelper/inducer T‑cells; binds MHC‑II; the major HIV receptor
CD832 kDaSuppressor/cytotoxic T‑cells; binds MHC‑I
CD45180–220 kDaLeukocyte Common Antigen — expressed on all leukocytes; used to gate the lymphocyte population on CD45 vs SSC before any subset analysis

CD4 and CD8 percentages are not perfectly exclusive: CD8 also reacts with NK cells, and CD4 also reacts with monocytes — which is exactly why the CD45/SSC lymphocyte gate has to be drawn correctly before either quadrant plot is read (§7).

3.2 · Clinical use

CD4+ counts monitor disease progression and treatment efficacy in HIV infection — CD4 testing remains central to the 90‑90‑90 HIV treatment targets, especially in HIV–tuberculosis co‑infected patients. CD8+ counts outside the reference range are associated with certain autoimmune conditions. More broadly, T‑lymphocyte subset testing is used for:

  • Direction and monitoring of infection, including more than two serial results to gauge treatment response
  • Immunodeficiency work-up — recurrent/severe infection, or before paediatric vaccination in a child with a suspicious history
  • Screening for haematological disease/lymphoma when prolonged treatment for another condition is not working as expected
  • Oncology — treatment-effectiveness monitoring and infection-risk assessment during therapy
  • Peri-operative infection-risk assessment
  • Monitoring patients on immunosuppressants for complications of low immunity
  • General health-screening / sub-health assessment

Who should be tested: patients with recurrent or severe infections or infections resistant to conventional treatment; patients with allergic, autoimmune or autoinflammatory disease; patients with haematological or solid tumours; patients with a family history of immunodeficiency; and patients on long-term immunosuppressive therapy.

3.3 · Specimen requirements

Anticoagulant

K2‑ or K3‑EDTA venous blood

Minimum volume

200 μL (follow the tube manufacturer’s own minimum fill line first)

Stain within

48 h of draw, stored at 20–25°C — do not refrigerate or freeze

Acquire within

24 h of staining

Ideal WBC concentration is (3–10)×10³ cells/μL — outside that range, concentrate or dilute the specimen first (a Mindray hematology analyzer WBC count, e.g. BC‑5800/BC‑5380, is the recommended check).

Interfering conditions

Refrigerated-before-staining blood, samples from patients on immunosuppressive drugs (poor population resolution), circulating blast cells, hyperbilirubinemia, chylous (lipemic) specimens and hemolysed specimens can all distort results. Reject and redraw grossly lipemic, jaundiced, haemolysed or clotted specimens rather than trying to salvage them.

3.4 · Instrument compatibility

Validated on Mindray BriCyte MX/MC/E6, BD FACSCalibur, BD FACSCanto II and BD FACSLyric; the cytometer must provide 488 nm and 635/638 nm excitation and four-colour (FITC/PE/PerCP/APC) detection of scatter plus fluorescence.

Sample prep & staining

The Lyse/No‑Wash protocol used for this panel — identical whether you learned it from the reagent’s IFU, the training deck, or the laboratory’s own SOP; the three agree step for step.

What you need

  • CD3‑FITC/CD8‑PE/CD45‑PerCP/CD4‑APC reagent, 12×75 mm flow tubes, EDTA whole blood
  • 1× Lysing Solution, freshly diluted 1:10 with distilled/deionized water (never isotonic saline or PBS — it will not lyse)
  • Vortex mixer, calibrated micropipettes (20 μL / 50 μL / 450 μL)
  1. Label one tube “T.” Pipette 20 μL of CD3‑FITC/CD8‑PE/CD45‑PerCP/CD4‑APC reagent into the bottom of the tube.
  2. Add blood. Pipette 50 μL of well‑mixed EDTA whole blood into the bottom of the tube — add the antibody first, then the blood, and avoid smearing it on the tube wall (wipe with a cotton swab if it happens). For absolute counting, invert the blood tube 10× before drawing, and consider reverse pipetting for accuracy.
  3. Mix & incubate #1. Gentle point-vortex 3–5×. Incubate 15 minutes at room temperature (20–25°C), protected from light.
  4. Lyse. Add 450 μL of freshly diluted 1× Lysing Solution. Gentle point-vortex 3–5×.
  5. Incubate #2. 10–15 minutes at room temperature, protected from light — the red cells lyse while leukocytes are preserved and fixed.
  6. Mix and run. Mix gently immediately before loading to prevent aggregation. If not tested immediately, hold in the dark at room temperature and complete acquisition within 24 h of staining.
Pre-treatment checklist
  • EDTA or heparin anticoagulant only — reject and redraw severe lipemia/jaundice/hemolysis/clots
  • Antibody goes into the tube first, blood second
  • Return antibody reagent to 2–8°C immediately after use
  • Dilute lysing solution with distilled water only — isotonic saline or PBS will not lyse red cells
  • If ambient temperature is low, incubate in a 37°C block or water bath (foil the tube mouth for a water bath)
  • Open/manual-loading samples must be mixed by hand immediately before the run

The companion BNK tube (for context)

Where the laboratory also runs the full T/B/NK panel, a second tube (“BNK”) is prepared the same way with CD3‑FITC/CD16+56‑PE/CD45‑PerCP/CD19‑APC reagent, 20 μL reagent + 50 μL blood, same incubation times. The two tubes are cross-checked against each other during review — see §7.2.

Lyse/Wash alternative (when a washed, fixed specimen is needed instead)
1Add 2 mL of 1× Lysing Solution to 100 μL blood+antibody; vortex; incubate 10 min, dark, room temp
2Centrifuge 5 min at 300×g; discard supernatant
3Resuspend in 2 mL PBS; repeat the centrifuge/discard step
4Resuspend the pellet in an appropriate buffer for analysis (fixed specimens are stable a few hours — analyse within 2 h if unfixed)
05

Running the test

The MRFlow software workflow, from power-on to shutdown, for the T‑Lymphocyte Subsets (CD3/8/45/4‑Auto) template.

5.1 · Start of day

  1. Power on the cytometer at the rear switch; confirm the status indicator settles to Ready (steady, not flickering). A blue/yellow or blue/red flicker means the software/hardware environment is abnormal — contact Mindray support rather than proceeding.
  2. Start the computer and open MRFlow (default password Admin — configure real user IDs under Settings per your laboratory’s access policy).
  3. Let the instrument complete its initialization sequence.
  4. Run daily QC before any patient sample — see §6.

5.2 · Placing a sample order

  1. On the Sample screen, select Sample Orders.
  2. Enter the Sample ID (auto-increments; max 25 characters including prefix). For batch autoload runs, set the tube position and order number.
  3. In Panels, choose T Lymphocyte Subsets (CD3/8/45/4‑Auto) — the software adds the tube automatically.
  4. Select Submit.

On a Mindray template you may only adjust Abs. Count settings, stop conditions/events and report text/reference ranges before the run completes — the gating logic itself is locked to keep results comparable across operators.

5.3 · Loading & acquisition

Manual loading

  1. Place the prepared tube on the sample probe holder.
  2. Select the tube, adjust voltage/threshold/compensation only if genuinely needed, and start acquisition.
  3. Watch the plots populate in real time; stop conditions (time, volume, or events in a set gate, combined as “OR”) end the run automatically.

Autoloading

  1. Load tubes into the carousel matching the work order, starting at the correct position — the system only reads tubes downstream of the designated start tube.
  2. Close the autoloader door and confirm the carousel seats correctly.
  3. Start the batch; the instrument aspirates, analyses and advances automatically.

Typical flow-rate settings: Low ≈10 μL/min (best resolution — use for QC beads), Medium ≈50 μL/min, High ≈100 μL/min (routine throughput).

5.4 · Reviewing and releasing

Once acquisition finishes, gates can still be dragged and the statistics recalculate live — do this before printing/transmitting, following the checklist in §7. Reports can be printed or sent directly to the LIS/HIS from the report preview screen.

5.5 · End of day / shutdown

  1. Select the shutdown icon in the top-right of the software.
  2. Prepare two tubes: ≥3 mL of diluted Probe Cleanser (1:10 in distilled water) in position 1, and ≥3 mL distilled water in position 2.
  3. Confirm to run the shutdown program — it completes automatically.
  4. Once the status indicator turns yellow, power off at the rear switch, empty the waste container appropriately, and power down the computer and monitor.
Note

After shutdown, wait at least 60 seconds before powering back on. Avoid cycling power repeatedly in a short window — it can damage the instrument.

06

Quality control

Two layers of QC: auto setup calibrates the instrument itself every day using QC fluorescent beads, and panel QC (whole blood QC) validates the entire process — staining, lysis, instrument and analysis — using an assigned-value blood control.

6.1 · Daily auto setup with QC beads

The QC particle is SPHERO™ Supra/Ultra Rainbow Fluorescent Particles (Mindray catalogue equivalent 105‑004485‑00, Spherotech URFP‑30‑2 / SRCP‑35‑2A, 3.0–3.5 μm, ≈10×10&sup6; particles/mL). Every particle carries a mixture of fluorophores excitable from 365–650 nm, so one bead lot can set every channel on every laser at once.

  1. Shake/vortex the bead vial. Add 1–2 drops into a tube with 1 mL sheath fluid; mix well.
  2. First time with a new lot: QC → QC Setup → QC Particles → New — scan the lot’s three QR codes, or enter the lot number, product ID and channel targets manually.
  3. Place the bead solution in the loading position. QC → Auto Setup; choose the lot; choose System Calibration + Voltage Calibration for a full setup, or Voltage Calibration alone for a quick recheck; start the test.
  4. Confirm every laser/channel shows a pass indicator on the report before running any patient sample. A repeated failure is escalated to Mindray for recalibration — do not analyse patient samples on a failed setup.
  5. Review the Levey‑Jennings trend under QC → QC Graph → QC Particles for long-term drift, not just today’s pass/fail.
Do not skip

Running a QC sample while an unrelated instrument error is showing produces unreliable calibration. Clear all errors first, then run QC.

6.2 · Panel (whole blood) QC

Stain and lyse the assigned-value blood control exactly as a patient sample (§4), run it under the matching QC panel template, and let the software plot the result against the control’s target value and limits on a Levey‑Jennings chart.

  1. QC → QC Setup → Whole Blood QC → New — enter control name, lot, expiry and product ID; choose the panel template; set target values/limits (or upper & lower limit) per parameter.
  2. Prepare and stain the control per its own instructions, using the same lyse/no-wash steps as §4.
  3. Place a QC sample order using the QC panel template, then run it exactly as a normal sample (§5).
  4. Adjust compensation/gates only if genuinely needed to correctly classify the populations.
  5. Review results under QC → QC Graph → Whole Blood QC.

6.3 · Levey‑Jennings / Westgard-style QC rules

07

Gate & result review

This is the checklist that turns raw dot plots into a defensible report — the same sequence the SOP and the training material both walk through, verbatim.

7.1 · Verify the gates, in order

  1. CD45 vs SSC (tube T). The lymphocyte gate must tightly and accurately encircle the lymphocyte cluster — low SSC, positive CD45, clearly separated from monocytes (higher SSC) and granulocytes (broader, highest SSC).
  2. CD3 vs CD4, and CD3 vs CD8 cross-gates. The horizontal line sits just above the double-negative and CD3-only clusters without cutting into either; the vertical line sits centred between the double-negative/CD3-only clusters and the single/double-positive clusters, with all four quadrants clearly separated.

7.2 · Internal consistency checks

  • T vs BNK tube CD3% agreement (where both tubes are run): should be within 5% of each other. A larger gap usually means a sample/tube mix-up, not a biological finding — re-check tube labelling before re-testing.
  • CD4% + CD8% vs CD3%: should agree within 5–10%. Beyond that threshold, work through the branching check below.
  • T+B+NK sum (T/B/NK panel): should total 95–105% in a normal individual; no prompt needed above 90%. Below 90%, inspect for an abnormal/atypical population and note its phenotype and percentage, or (if none is visible) note a CD3–CD19–CD16–CD56– population. The automated Mindray algorithm will not itself report a sum above 105%.
  • First-plot screen. Compare the very first plot of each tube (typically FSC/SSC or CD45/SSC) against the normal reference image. More than 10% of events sitting in the primitive/blast-cell region is reported as a peripheral-blood blast-cell suspicion, not silently passed through.

7.3 · When CD45/SSC is clean but the CD3/CD4/CD8 plots are not

If lymphocytes gate cleanly on CD45 vs SSC but the downstream CD3‑CD4 / CD3‑CD8 plots show poor, disorderly separation, the cause is usually residual protein/debris interfering with antibody resolution — wash and re-suspend the tube:

  1. Add 2 mL sheath fluid to the prepared T (and BNK) tube; mix gently on the vortex.
  2. Centrifuge at 1,500 rpm for 5 minutes.
  3. Remove the supernatant in one pour — never a second pour, and never shake the tube while decanting.
  4. Add 500 μL sheath fluid to resuspend the pellet, then re-run on the instrument.
After a wash step

The instrument’s absolute count from a washed tube is no longer reliable — report absolute counts using the CBC lymphocyte count × the flow cytometry percentage instead: Lymphocyte count (CBC) × subset %.

7.4 · Release checklist

  • Gates verified per §7.1 on every tube
  • Internal consistency checks per §7.2 within tolerance, or an explanatory note added
  • No unexplained blast/primitive-cell signal >10%
  • QC for the day is in control (§6)

Only once all four are satisfied should the report be printed or transmitted to LIS/HIS.

08

HLA-B27 add-on

Runs on the same BriCyte hardware and lysing solution as the T-cell panel, with its own two-colour reagent and interpretation logic — worth knowing well since it’s frequently ordered alongside, or instead of, the T-cell panel in the same laboratories.

8.1 · What it is and who orders it

HLA‑B27 is a class I MHC gene product expressed on all nucleated cells, especially abundant on lymphocytes. A positive result does not itself diagnose disease; a negative result largely rules out ankylosing spondylitis.

Positivity rate by condition

Clinically: a negative test largely excludes ankylosing spondylitis; a positive test prompts the clinician to look for additional spondyloarthropathy features before confirming the diagnosis.

8.2 · Sample prep

  1. Two tubes. Tube 1 (isotype control): 20 μL IgG2a‑FITC/IgG1‑PE. Tube 2 (test): 20 μL HLA‑B27‑FITC/HLA‑B7‑PE.
  2. Add 100 μL mixed EDTA venous blood to each tube (avoid the tube wall; wipe with a swab if it happens).
  3. Vortex gently; incubate 15 min at room temperature (20–25°C), dark.
  4. Add 2 mL of freshly diluted 1× lysing solution to each tube; vortex gently; incubate 10 min, room temperature, dark (avoid prolonging this step — extended hemolysin contact can destroy leukocytes).
  5. Centrifuge 1,500 rpm × 5 min; discard supernatant. Add 2 mL sheath fluid, vortex gently to resuspend, centrifuge again 1,500 rpm × 5 min; discard supernatant.
  6. Resuspend in 500 μL sheath fluid. Test within 2 hours.
Wash chemistry

Lysing must be done with distilled water — never isotonic saline or PBS. The wash step, by contrast, must use an isotonic solution (sheath fluid, saline or PBS) — never plain water.

8.3 · Reading the result

  1. Review both FSC‑SSC plots first: lymphocytes must be clearly demarcated from debris, and the gate must accurately circle the lymphocyte cluster, in both the isotype-control tube and the sample tube.
  2. Check the reported MFI shift against the reagent’s cutoff (MFI cutoff 5,400; grey zone 4,860–5,940, i.e. ±10% of cutoff).
  3. Negative — release as reported.
  4. Positive — go back to the sample-tube HLA‑B27/HLA‑B7 plot:
    • Q4 (B27‑single‑positive) > 80% → report positive.
    • Q4 < 80% with double-positive cells present in Q2 → report positive, and note possible HLA‑B7 cross-reactivity; recommend genetic confirmation.
    • Q4 < 80% with cells present in Q1 → report positive, note possible interference, recommend genetic confirmation.
  5. Result in the grey zone (MFI difference within cutoff ±10%) — report the actual value with a note that it falls in the reagent’s grey zone and recommend correlating with another methodology.
  6. “Undetermined” or blank result — lymphocytes were not separated from debris on the scatter plot:
    • If they can be manually re-gated in FSC‑SSC, re-circle the lymphocyte population in both tubes and the result will resolve.
    • If they genuinely cannot be separated, re-prep the sample: check whether the tube looks reddish (incomplete hemolysis — revisit the lysing step), or whether lymphocyte/monocyte/granulocyte numbers look normal on FSC‑SSC (if abnormally low, hemolysis time was too long and destroyed leukocytes — shorten it). For a same-day sample, adjust and re-run; for an older sample, reduce hemolysis time and consider washing more than once.

Troubleshooting

Every hardware error on BriCyte follows the same three-step ladder: Remove & retry → Restart the cytometer → Contact Mindray Customer Service. What differs is which specific check to make first. Errors group into three families by their code prefix.

9.1 · Fluidics & sample errors (2xxxx)

SymptomFirst check

Flow sensor abnormalCheck sample volume is sufficient and free of excess bubbles, re-run; if it recurs, run Clean Flow Cell 1–3×.
Sample probe / flow cell cloggedCheck for particulates or bubbles in the sample; run Unclog Sampling Channel or Unclog Flow Cell 1–3×.
Sheath tubing emptyCheck the sheath inlet connector on the reagent carriage is tight; run Prime Bubble Filter 1–3×.
Insufficient sheath / waste container fullReplace the sheath container, or empty the waste container — do not try to run the instrument past either alarm.
Ambient / sheath temperature abnormalConfirm room temperature is within 15–32°C before retrying.

9.2 · Optics & laser errors (32xxx / 33xxx)

Laser and APD-detector-box temperature/power errors (red, blue and violet lasers each have their own set of codes) share one first check: confirm ambient temperature is within 15–32°C, click Remove, and retry. If a laser power or temperature error persists after a restart, it needs Mindray service — do not attempt to open the optical box beyond confirming it is fully closed (an “optical box open” error is the one case that’s user-resolvable: close it and retry).

9.3 · Loading / mechanical errors (4xxxx)

Unloading or carousel-handling errors: Remove & retry, then restart; check the carousel is seated correctly and no tube is obstructing the tube-access door before calling for service.

Reading an error

The error dialog always shows the most recent error first, with its own troubleshooting text; select any listed error to see its specific instructions. “Mute Alarm” only silences the beep — it does not clear the error. “Remove” clears errors that are auto-clearable; anything else needs the listed corrective action first.

Recommended: 75% ethyl alcohol. Prohibited: 3% hydrogen peroxide (corrodes metal), Aerodesin 2000, Cidex OPA. Wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection — every surface and component is treated as potentially infectious.

The System → Log screen keeps 2 years of error and operation logs (oldest overwritten once full) — filterable by date, error ID/level, or log type, and exportable to PC. 11.2 · Lysing solution

10× concentrate; active ingredients diethylene glycol (≤40%) and formaldehyde (≤15%) — treat as hazardous (H302/H312/H314/H317/H332/H351: harmful if swallowed/in contact with skin/inhaled, causes severe skin and eye burns, may cause an allergic skin reaction, suspected carcinogen). Dilute 1:10 with distilled/deionized water immediately before use (10× concentrate : water = 1:9). Diluted 1× solution is stable 30 days at 20–25°C. Concentrate: store 2–25°C, unopened shelf life 2 years, opened stability 6 months.

Two protocols exist: Lyse/No‑Wash (450 μL 1× solution into 50 μL blood+antibody, 15 min dark incubation, run directly — this is the protocol used for the routine CD3/CD4/CD8/CD45 panel) and Lyse/Wash (2 mL 1× solution into 100 μL blood+antibody, 10 min incubation, centrifuge/wash twice with PBS — used where a washed, more stable preparation is needed). 11.3 · Sheath fluid

An electrically conducting, fluorescence-free buffer (phosphate buffer ≤0.5%, sodium chloride ≤1%, preservative ≤0.3%) that forms the carrier stream for every sample. Store 2–32°C; unopened shelf life 2 years; opened stability 60 days at 15–32°C. Never freeze. If blank-count results look abnormal after shipping, let the container sit at room temperature 24 h before use.

11.4 · QC particles

SPHERO™ Rainbow Fluorescent Particles, single population, 3.0–3.5 μm, ≈10×10&sup6; particles/mL in deionized water with 0.02% sodium azide / 0.01% NP40, excitable 365–650 nm. Diluted particles keep refrigerated but expire one year after the vial is first opened — write the opening date on the bottle.

11.5 · Probe cleanser

Dilute 1:10 with distilled water before use. Used during the Clean Flow Cell maintenance routine and at shutdown (≥3 mL per cycle).

12

Lab-user FAQ

The questions hospital lab staff actually ask on a support call, answered from the source documentation.

“My CD4%+CD8% doesn’t add up to CD3%. Is the machine broken?”

Not necessarily. A 5–10% deviation is expected and normal. Beyond that, it’s a signal to look at the CD4–CD8 plot for an atypical population (double-positive or double-negative cells) before assuming an instrument fault — see §7.2.

“Can I use PBS or saline instead of distilled water to dilute the lysing solution?”

No. Isotonic solutions will not lyse red cells — the lysing step specifically depends on the hypotonic shock from distilled/deionized water. Use isotonic solutions only for the wash step (sheath fluid, saline or PBS), never for lysing.

“Do I need to run QC beads before every batch, or just once a day?”

Once per day, before the first patient sample — but re-run it any time you have reason to doubt the instrument (an error was just cleared, a filter was replaced, a laser fault occurred), not only on a fixed clock.

“The plots are diagonal-smeared instead of square. What’s wrong?”

That’s the visual signature of a compensation problem, not a biological or gating one. Re-run QC/auto setup with fresh beads and check the compensation matrix before re-gating anything.

“Can I refrigerate the blood sample before staining if I’m running behind?”

No — refrigeration before staining is specifically called out as a source of aberrant results. Keep the specimen at room temperature (20–25°C) and stain within 48 h of draw.

“Why is my HLA-B27 result showing ‘Undetermined’?”

The software couldn’t separate lymphocytes from debris on the FSC‑SSC plot. Try manually re-circling the lymphocyte population first; if that’s genuinely not possible, check whether the tube looks reddish (incomplete lysis) or whether cell counts look unusually low (hemolysis ran too long) — see §8.3.

“A patient’s blood is very lipemic/jaundiced/haemolysed — should I run it anyway?”

No. These are explicitly listed interfering conditions that can yield incorrect results (bilirubin and chylomicrons both interfere with the assay). Request a redraw where clinically possible rather than reporting an unreliable result.

“Can I mix reagents or QC material from different manufacturers on this instrument?”

No — the cytometer, reagents and controls are validated as one system. Mixed-supplier use is explicitly called out as a cause of degraded performance and unreliable results.

“Why does the lab need to set its own reference range instead of using the one in this guide?”

The published ranges (§3.5) come from one population (China, ages 18–65) using one preparative technique. Gender, age, ethnicity and local technique can all shift where “normal” sits — the manufacturer explicitly asks each laboratory to verify or establish its own range rather than adopt the insert value unchanged.
